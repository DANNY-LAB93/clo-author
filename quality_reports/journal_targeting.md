**One number decides the shortlist before any question of scope or fit.** Every
serious microbiology or infectious-diseases journal caps a systematic review
between roughly 3,500 and 4,500 words. This manuscript is **four to six times
over**, and the excess is not padding: Results and Methods together are 17,000
words because the review documents its own corrections, its failed classifiers,
and its rejected models.

That material is the paper's chief asset in review and its chief liability at
submission. It has to move to supplementary files, not be deleted — and that is
a restructuring job, not a copy-edit.

## What is actually being submitted

The Q1 dual-expertise referee report (`2026-08-03_referee_q1_micro_stats.md`)
concluded Major Revision, and argued against reclassification as a scoping
review, on the grounds that the paper's most valuable results are quantitative:
the eradication mixture artefact (τ² 1.995 → exactly 0.000 on the airway/non-airway
split) and the classification-sensitivity collapse (MDR clinical success: 17
studies at author-reported labels, 2 studies and 3 patients verified).

**Target the paper on those**, not on the pooled proportions. A submission whose
headline is "78.7% clinical success in phage therapy" invites the rejection the
referee already wrote: 33 of 40 arms carry one patient, GRADE is Very low in all
13 cells, and the only randomised evidence is null. A submission whose headline
is "resistance classification does not survive verification, and eradication is
two constructs" is a methodological contribution that stands on its own.

## Shortlist

### First choice — Clinical Microbiology and Infection (CMI)

ESCMID's journal, Q1, and the natural home for a review that is simultaneously
about *P. aeruginosa* resistance classification and about evidence synthesis.
Routinely publishes systematic reviews; its readership is exactly the clinical
microbiologists who will recognise why the Magiorakos-versus-verified collapse
matters. Expect hard scrutiny of the meta-analysis of uncontrolled proportions —
which the manuscript is now equipped to answer, since Table 6 has been reframed
as evidence *for* the GLMM rather than as unexplained instability.

### Equal first — Journal of Antimicrobial Chemotherapy (JAC)

Same tier, AMR-focused readership, takes systematic reviews. Marginally more
tolerant of a methods-forward framing. Choose between CMI and JAC on audience:
CMI leans clinical microbiology, JAC leans antimicrobial therapy and stewardship.

### The strategic option — International Journal of Antimicrobial Agents (IJAA)

**IJAA published Liu et al. 2025**, the 130-study individual-patient-data phage
meta-analysis that this review cites, screens against, and partially disagrees
with. That cuts both ways and both should be weighed rather than assumed.

*In favour:* the editor knows this literature better than any other editor will,
the referee pool is already assembled, and a paper that examines what the prior
synthesis could not — resistance-label verification, construct validity of
eradication — lands naturally where that synthesis was published.

*Against:* the journal has already invested in a competing synthesis, and this
review is in part a critique of the assumptions behind that class of analysis. If
Liu's authors referee it, the exchange will be substantive but not gentle.

### Realistic and respectable

- **Clinical Infectious Diseases** — Green 2023 is here
- **Open Forum Infectious Diseases** — open access, IDSA; Aslam 2020 and Cesta 2023 are here
- **Journal of Infection** — takes methodologically demanding reviews
- **Infection and Drug Resistance** — Lévêque 2023 is here

### High-acceptance, and honest about the trade-off

**Viruses** and **Antibiotics** (MDPI) impose no hard word limit, publish
phage-therapy work routinely, and hold three and one of this corpus's own studies
respectively. Submitting there would avoid the 20,639-word problem entirely.

It would also place a methodological critique of the field in venues the field
does not treat as arbiters. If the goal is that the classification-collapse
finding changes practice, this is the wrong choice. If the goal is publication
for a master's-thesis requirement, it is a legitimate one. **State which goal
applies before choosing — the decision is not technical.**

## What must happen before any of these

**1. Cut the main text to ~4,000 words.** Move to supplementary: the search
architecture and per-database counts, the stage-1 screening rules and their three
rejected alternatives, the model comparison table, the falsification analyses, the
provenance audit of the local document corpus. Keep in the main text: PRISMA flow,
corpus description, the two decisive quantitative findings, GRADE, limitations.

**2. Restructure the abstract.** 140 words is under CMI's and JAC's limits, but
both require a *structured* abstract (Objectives / Methods / Results /
Conclusions) of roughly 250 words. It also currently leads with pooled
proportions; it should lead with the verification findings.

**3. Resolve the two open non-technical items**, both of which every journal on
this list will ask about at submission: PROSPERO registration, and whether
screening was performed by two independent reviewers. The Rayyan project is
configured for blind dual screening but a second reviewer has not been invited.

**4. Declare the searched sources and the unsearched ones.** Eight sources
executed with verified counts; Embase, Web of Science and EBSCO absent with the
reason recorded. Reviewers at this tier check Embase specifically — the honest
statement that it is not in the institutional subscription, paired with the
demonstration that CENTRAL supplies Embase-sourced trial records, is defensible.
An unexplained absence is not.

## What not to do

**Do not restrict the search or the corpus to microbiology journals.** Measured
against this corpus: a microbiology/ID journal filter removes **26 of 42 included
studies**, and a strict microbiology-only filter leaves about **7**. Clinical
phage therapy publishes where the patient is — transplantation, otolaryngology,
respiratory medicine, paediatrics — not where the organism is. Filtering on venue
selects for a variable unrelated to eligibility, which is the same error as the
mandatory resistance block already corrected.

The journal *distribution* is worth reporting as a descriptive result. The journal
*filter* would destroy the review.
