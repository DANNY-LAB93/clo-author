# Research Specification: Phage Therapy for MDR/XDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — A Stratified Proportion Meta-Analysis

**Revision history:** Originally scoped as a comparative-effect meta-analysis (phage+antibiotic vs. antibiotic monotherapy, comparative studies only). `/discover lit` (two rounds, see [librarian reports](literature/phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas/)) found this design infeasible — 0-1 qualifying comparative studies exist. Revised 2026-07-18 to a **meta-analysis of proportions**, per user direction. See [Decision Record](decisions/discovery_phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas.md) for the full pivot rationale.

## Research Question
Among patients with MDR/XDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* infections treated with bacteriophage therapy, what is the pooled proportion of clinical success and the pooled proportion of safety events — and how do these vary by resistance category (MDR vs. XDR), route of phage administration, and treatment modality (antibiotic monotherapy vs. phage-antibiotic combination)?

## Motivation
MDR/XDR *P. aeruginosa* is a WHO priority pathogen with a shrinking antibiotic pipeline. The phage therapy evidence base for this pathogen is dominated by single-arm case series/cohorts and a handful of trials with non-matching comparators (see literature search reports) — too sparse in comparative (dual-arm) design to support a pooled effect-size (RR/OR) meta-analysis, but abundant enough in single-arm form to support a **proportion meta-analysis**: pooling the rate of clinical success and safety events across studies/regimens, stratified by clinically meaningful factors, rather than forcing a paired causal comparison the data cannot support.

## Hypothesis
Pooled clinical success proportions will be high across phage-containing regimens (monotherapy and combination), reflecting the salvage-therapy context of most included studies (patients selected for phage therapy after antibiotic failure) — this ceiling/selection effect must be treated as a limitation of interpretation, not evidence that phage therapy "works" in a causal sense. Whether the combination (phage+antibiotic) stratum shows a materially higher pooled success proportion than the antibiotic-monotherapy stratum (where such data exist) is an open, testable question — not assumed a priori, given the design's inability to support causal claims.

## Empirical Strategy (preliminary)
- **Design:** Systematic review + **meta-analysis of proportions** per PRISMA 2020. Eligibility **broadened** relative to the original design: both single-arm studies (case series, cohorts, compassionate-use reports) and comparative studies are eligible, since proportions are computed per arm/regimen rather than as a paired within-study effect size. This resolves the feasibility failure of the original comparative-only design.
- **Population definition:** MDR and XDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* infections, classified per **Magiorakos et al. (2012)** criteria where derivable from reported susceptibility data; otherwise per the original study's own MDR/XDR classification (documented per-study as a risk-of-bias/heterogeneity note).
- **Outcomes:**
  - **Clinical success** (primary) — composite of clinical cure/improvement as defined by the primary study; per-study definition extracted and tabulated given expected heterogeneity in how "success" is defined across the literature.
  - **Safety** (primary) — proportion of patients experiencing ≥1 adverse event; serious adverse events extracted as a secondary stratum where reported.
  - **Secondary:** microbiological eradication, mortality, length of hospitalization, resistance emergence during treatment (retained from the original PICO where reported).
- **Stratification / subgroup analysis (pre-specified):**
  1. MDR vs. XDR
  2. Route of phage administration (topical/local, intravenous, inhaled/nebulized, oral, intra-articular, other)
  3. Treatment modality — antibiotic monotherapy vs. phage-antibiotic combination (and phage monotherapy, if reported as a distinct arm/regimen)
- **Pooling model:** Random-effects meta-analysis of proportions, using a variance-stabilizing transformation appropriate for proportions near the 0/1 boundary (Freeman-Tukey double arcsine, with logit-transformation as a sensitivity comparison), back-transformed for reporting. Random-effects chosen a priori given expected clinical and methodological heterogeneity.
- **Risk of bias:** ROBINS-I for comparative non-randomized studies; a case-series-appropriate tool (e.g., Murad et al. 2018 case series critical appraisal, or the JBI checklist for case series/case reports) for single-arm studies; Cochrane RoB2 for any RCTs identified.
- **Heterogeneity:** I² and τ² reported for every pooled proportion; subgroup meta-analysis (not meta-regression on a comparative effect size, since there is no paired effect size to regress) for the three stratification variables above.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Leave-one-out; transformation-choice sensitivity (arcsine vs. logit); publication bias assessment (funnel plot, Egger's-type test adapted for proportions) if ≥10 studies per stratum.

## Data
- **Unit of observation:** Study-arm-level proportions (numerator = patients with outcome event, denominator = patients in that arm/regimen), extracted per study, per treatment modality, where multiple regimens are reported within a single study.
- **Sources:** PubMed/MEDLINE (searched directly via E-Utilities API, see literature reports), ClinicalTrials.gov/WHO ICTRP, and — pending — EMBASE, Web of Science, Scopus (user to run personally with institutional access; adapted Boolean queries already provided).
- **Key variables needed per study-arm:** treatment modality (monotherapy vs. combination, and antibiotic vs. phage-only where distinguishable), MDR/XDR classification method, clinical success definition and count, adverse event count, microbiological eradication definition, mortality, hospitalization length, resistance emergence, phage administration route/regimen, sample size per arm.

## Expected Results
High pooled clinical success proportions overall (salvage-therapy selection effect expected to inflate this regardless of true efficacy — an explicit interpretive caveat, not a finding to be taken at face value). The comparative value of this design is in the **stratified breakdown** (MDR vs. XDR, route, monotherapy vs. combination) rather than a single headline pooled number.

## Contribution
Per the literature search, 8-9 systematic reviews/meta-analyses of phage therapy have been published since 2019 (most recently Liu et al. 2025, IJAA, an IPD meta-analysis of 130 studies), so a "since 2019" temporal gap framing does not hold (see Decision Record). This review's contribution is instead: **the first synthesis to (a) stratify pooled outcome proportions explicitly by MDR vs. XDR resistance category for *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* specifically, and (b) report clinical success and safety separately by route of administration and by monotherapy-vs-combination modality** — a level of stratification not present in the broader, multi-pathogen, non-stratified pooling done by Liu et al. (2025) or the narrative approach of Uyttebroek et al. (2022) (to be confirmed once full text of these anchor papers is obtained — see Open Questions).

## Open Questions
1. **Anchor paper differentiation:** Full text of Liu et al. (2025, IJAA) and Uyttebroek et al. (2022, Lancet ID) needs review to confirm neither already performs this exact MDR/XDR-stratified, Pseudomonas-specific proportion analysis — currently based on abstract-level evidence only.
2. **EMBASE/Web of Science/Scopus:** User is running these searches personally (institutional access required); results pending. No longer a feasibility-blocking gate under the proportion-meta-analysis design, but still valuable for completeness (more single-arm studies = more precise pooled estimates; forward-citation analysis may surface additional MDR/XDR-stratified data).
3. **MDR/XDR reclassification risk:** Many primary studies report "MDR" without full susceptibility panel data needed to independently verify Magiorakos et al. (2012) criteria — expect a meaningful fraction of studies to be classified by author-report only, a limitation to document explicitly (and possibly a sensitivity analysis: author-reported vs. independently-verified classification).
4. **Route-of-administration granularity:** Need to confirm during data extraction whether enough studies per route category exist for a meaningful stratified estimate, or whether some route categories will need to be collapsed (e.g., "topical/local" combining wound irrigation and dressing-based delivery).
5. **Monotherapy stratum sparsity:** Antibiotic-monotherapy-only regimen data, as a distinct arm/proportion (not a comparator to combination therapy), may still be sparse within phage therapy studies (most patients in this literature receive some phage-containing regimen by definition of study inclusion) — clarify during `/discover data`/extraction whether monotherapy-only proportions can be meaningfully estimated from this literature at all, or whether that stratum instead needs external benchmark data from separate antibiotic-treatment-outcome studies (a design choice for `/strategize`).

## Open Questions
1. **Feasibility risk:** Is there a sufficient volume of comparative (dual-arm) studies on adjuvant phage therapy vs. monotherapy in MDR *P. aeruginosa* specifically? If too few are found, the design may need to revert to including single-arm studies (with narrative synthesis) or pivot to a scoping review. Flagged for `/discover lit` and `/discover data` to quantify.
2. **Identify the 2019 review:** The librarian must identify the specific systematic review/meta-analysis of phage therapy published around 2019 that this review updates — needed to (a) confirm no updated synthesis has appeared since, (b) use its search strategy/scope as a baseline, and (c) validate that a 2019 cutoff for the new search is appropriate rather than a gap in the researcher's own literature awareness.
3. **Confounding by indication in comparative studies:** Even within dual-arm comparative studies, patients selected for adjuvant phage therapy may systematically differ (e.g., more refractory infections) from those on monotherapy. Analytical handling of this (matching, adjustment, or explicit limitation in Discussion) needs to be decided during `/strategize`.
4. **Pathogen specificity:** Some comparative studies on phage-antibiotic combination therapy may not be *Pseudomonas*-specific (mixed-organism cohorts). Eligibility criteria need a clear rule for how such studies are handled (exclude, or include with subgroup extraction if *Pseudomonas* data reported separately).
