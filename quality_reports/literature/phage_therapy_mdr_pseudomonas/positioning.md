# Positioning — Adjuvant Phage Therapy vs. Antibiotic Monotherapy in MDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*

**Note on scope:** Per the librarian's role, this file identifies where the project's planned design does and does not fit the literature landscape found. It does not propose an identification/evidence-synthesis strategy (that is the strategist's job) — it flags what the literature search implies for the two premises the project's contribution statement currently rests on.

---

## Does the 2019 Cutoff Hold Up? NO — Requires Revision

The research spec's search window (2019–present) and its contribution framing ("no phage therapy review since 2019... this review updates the evidence synthesis with literature published 2019-present") rest on the premise that El Haddad et al. (2019, *Clinical Infectious Diseases*) is the last systematic review of this literature. This search confirms El Haddad (2019) is very likely the specific paper the researcher remembers — but it is demonstrably **not** the last review. At minimum:

- **Uyttebroek et al. (2022), Lancet Infectious Diseases** — a large, high-impact systematic review with search coverage through August 2021 — directly supersedes El Haddad (2019) in recency and is in a comparably prestigious venue.
- **Liu et al. (2025), International Journal of Antimicrobial Agents** — an individual-patient-data meta-analysis covering literature through May 2025 (a few months before this search was run) — is both the most recent and the most methodologically ambitious (an actual pooled meta-analysis, not a narrative review). **Round 2: fully verified via PubMed (PMID 40633848) — see below.**
- Four additional reviews (Aranaga 2022, Al-Ishaq 2020/21, Ibrahim 2025, Yan 2026) further crowd the "systematic review of phage therapy" space post-2019.
- **Round 2: two more confirmed via direct PubMed querying (Terlizzi et al. 2026, PMID 41881246; Terzi et al. 2026, PMID 41887374) — see below.**

**Recommended revision:** The search cutoff should be updated to use **Liu et al. (2025)** as the new baseline/anchor (the most recent comprehensive synthesis), not El Haddad (2019). If the project still wants a defensible "gap" framing, it should be reframed around a **design gap**, not a **temporal gap**: *"no existing synthesis — including the most recent, May 2025 meta-analysis — restricts its evidence base to comparative (dual-arm) studies with a genuine antibiotic-monotherapy comparator, and none provide an MDR-*Pseudomonas*-specific comparative estimate."* This is a defensible, narrower, and more accurate contribution claim than "first update since 2019," and it survives even though the temporal premise does not.

**Action needed before `/strategize`:** The orchestrator/user should decide whether to (a) revise the contribution statement now, and (b) extend the search date range backward to ensure full coverage between the newly-identified 2022/2025 reviews and today, rather than defaulting to 2019.

## Does the Comparative-Only Design Still Make Sense? NOT AS CURRENTLY SCOPED — Feasibility Gate Fails (Round 2: conclusion holds, more rigorously documented)

Per `quality_reports/decisions/discovery_phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas.md`, the comparative-only design was explicitly conditioned on finding **at least ~5-10 comparative (dual-arm) studies**. This search found:

- **Zero** retrospective or prospective cohort studies with both a phage+antibiotic arm and an antibiotic-monotherapy arm for MDR *P. aeruginosa*.
- **One** RCT (PhagoBurn/Jault 2019) with a within-study comparator, but the comparator is standard wound care (not a clean antibiotic-monotherapy arm), the population is burn-wound infection generally (not MDR-defined), and the phage arm underperformed the comparator.
- **Three** CF trials (AP-PA02, BX004-A, TP-102) with placebo (not antibiotic-monotherapy) comparators, population/outcome mismatches. **Round 2 update: all three now have confirmed, peer-reviewed or registry-posted primary results** (BX004-A: Nature Communications, PMID 40593506; TP-102: Med, PMID 39740667; AP-PA02: ClinicalTrials.gov structured results, NCT04596319, hasResults=true) — this resolves the Round 1 "unverified/press-release-only" flag but does not change their PICO-ineligibility (still placebo comparators, not antibiotic monotherapy; still CF chronic-colonization population, not necessarily MDR-defined). **Round 3 update: a fourth trial of the same design class, Stanley et al. (2025)/CYPHY (YPT-01, The Microbe 6:100254), is now also catalogued — see below.**

This is **far below the 5-10 study threshold** and squarely triggers the decision record's own invalidation clause: *"If `/discover lit` and `/discover data` find too few comparative studies... this design must be revisited."* **This original comparative-only design has since been superseded (2026-07-18) by a meta-analysis-of-proportions design** (see `quality_reports/research_spec_phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas.md`), which the Round 3 screen below evaluates against.

### Round 2 (PRISMA-Grade Follow-Up, 2026-07-18) — Does This Conclusion Survive More Rigorous Search?

**Yes — the conclusion survives, and is now backed by documented, reproducible search methodology rather than a web-search-only pass.** Full query strings, hit counts, and screening are recorded in `annotated_bibliography.md` under "PRISMA-Grade Follow-Up Search (Round 2)." Summary:

- **Four PubMed E-Utilities Boolean queries** (documented exact strings, 31 + 1 + 1 + 21 = 54 raw hits, all individually screened) surfaced **zero new qualifying human comparative studies** (dual-arm, phage+antibiotic vs. antibiotic-monotherapy, MDR *P. aeruginosa*). The single hit from the critic-specified negative-claim query (matched cohort / propensity score / registry / case-control terms) was a confirmed false positive (a 2016 conference-abstract-supplement index page, not a primary study).
- **ClinicalTrials.gov structured API v2 queries** (2 queries, 8 + 2 studies returned, all screened) confirmed no new completed comparative trial, but surfaced **one materially important new registered trial**:
  - **NCT07698002 ("DePHEAT-PJI")** — a pilot 1:1 randomized feasibility trial (Ottawa Hospital Research Institute) with a **genuine antibiotics-alone control arm** (standard DAIR + culture-directed antibiotics vs. same + adjunctive phage), for *S. aureus* or *P. aeruginosa* periprosthetic joint infection. This is the **closest PICO match found in either search round** — but it is NOT_YET_RECRUITING (estimated start July 2026, results not expected before 2027-2028), is a tiny feasibility pilot (n=10), and is **not MDR-restricted**. It contributes **zero current evidence** to the comparative-study count, but is an important forward-looking signal: another research group has independently arrived at the same design gap this project targets, which (a) validates that the gap is real and recognized, and (b) creates a multi-year watch-item / mild future scooping risk that should be tracked, not a reason to abandon the project now. **Round 3 note: this is no longer the single "closest PICO match" claim without qualification — see Leitner et al. (2021) below, which has a real (already-completed) antibiotic-monotherapy arm, albeit without a combination arm and without MDR/Pseudomonas specificity.**
- **PROSPERO**: direct database querying was blocked (HTTP 403, JS/session-gated); a web-search-mediated indirect check found no competing protocol matching this project's exact PICO, but this is a **weaker, partial verification** than the PubMed queries above — it should be re-run by a user with direct PROSPERO access before final registration.
- **WHO ICTRP**: search portal is ASP.NET-postback-driven and could not be queried via static GET/WebFetch; only an indirect web-search check was possible, which found no registrations beyond what ClinicalTrials.gov already surfaced. Non-U.S./non-EU trial registries (ChiCTR, CTRI, ANZCTR) remain unchecked.
- **EMBASE, Web of Science, Scopus: NOT SEARCHED in either round.** This is an explicit, disclosed limitation requiring the user's own institutional access — not a gap this agent can close. It is the single most significant remaining source of uncertainty around the "0-1 comparative studies" conclusion, particularly for non-English-language studies and conference-abstract-only data that PubMed/ClinicalTrials.gov do not index.

**Bottom line for the pivot decision:** The feasibility gate still fails as currently scoped. Nothing in this more rigorous pass overturns the conclusion that MDR-*Pseudomonas*-aeruginosa-specific, phage-vs-antibiotic-monotherapy comparative evidence is essentially absent (0-1 studies, depending on how strictly "antibiotic monotherapy" is defined against PhagoBurn's standard-of-care comparator). The one new finding (DePHEAT-PJI) makes the case that this exact evidence gap is now recognized well enough that at least one other group is running a trial to fill it — which arguably *strengthens* the argument that documenting the gap itself is a genuine, citable contribution (see Suggested Contribution Statement below), while also flagging that this window may close in 2-3 years once DePHEAT-PJI or similar trials report.

### Options for the User/Orchestrator to Consider (not a recommendation — this is the strategist's call, flagged here only because it gates whether `/strategize` can proceed with the current design)

1. **Broaden pathogen scope** from "MDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* only" to "MDR Gram-negative organisms" or "ESKAPE pathogens," pooling the handful of comparative studies across pathogens and reporting a Pseudomonas subgroup analysis (likely underpowered, but transparent about it).
2. **Broaden comparator definition** to include placebo-on-background-antibiotics designs (the CF trials, now all fully verified peer-reviewed/registry-posted per Round 2) as PICO-eligible "monotherapy-equivalent" comparators, with explicit justification and sensitivity analysis excluding them.
3. **Revert to a scoping review / narrative synthesis** rather than a meta-analysis with pooled effect sizes — explicitly reporting that the comparative evidence base does not support pooling, and instead systematically cataloguing the single-arm evidence with a structured risk-of-bias discussion of why comparative estimates cannot yet be produced. This would still be a genuine, citable contribution (a "state of the evidence" paper), just not a meta-analysis. Note: Terzi et al. (2026) has already published a scoping review of inhaled phage therapy for MDR respiratory pathogens (including Pseudomonas) using essentially this format — differentiation from that paper would be needed if this option is chosen.
4. **Reframe the primary contribution as identifying and quantifying the evidence gap itself** — i.e., the paper's contribution becomes "we systematically searched PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, and (pending institutional access) EMBASE/WoS/Scopus and found that, despite eight-to-nine systematic reviews since 2019 and thousands of case reports, only 0-1 studies provide a genuine within-study comparator for phage-vs-antibiotic-monotherapy in MDR *Pseudomonas*, though a registered pilot trial (DePHEAT-PJI) suggests the field is beginning to address this design gap" — this is a defensible and increasingly common format in evidence-based medicine ("umbrella review" / "evidence map" papers) and does not require inventing a pooled effect size from too few studies.
5. **Proceed to `/discover data` regardless**, since a formal PRISMA-compliant database search in EMBASE/Cochrane CENTRAL (not yet done in either round — see explicit gap below) might surface additional grey-literature or non-English comparative studies not indexed in PubMed/ClinicalTrials.gov. Round 2 strengthens confidence in the "0-1" count but does **not** eliminate the need for this step before final publication.

**Note: as of 2026-07-18, the project's actual design has already moved to option approximating a hybrid of (2) and (4) above** — a meta-analysis of proportions (see `quality_reports/research_spec_phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas.md` and `quality_reports/decisions/discovery_phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas.md`), which sidesteps the comparative-only feasibility failure by pooling single-arm and comparative study proportions together, stratified rather than pooled into a single effect size. The options list above is retained for historical/audit purposes.

### Suggested Contribution Statement (draft language for the writer to adapt, not finalized strategy — updated post-Round-2)

> "Despite eight-to-nine systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and scoping reviews of bacteriophage therapy published since 2019 — most recently an individual-patient-data meta-analysis of 130 studies (Liu et al., 2025) and a scoping review of inhaled phage therapy for multidrug-resistant respiratory pathogens (Terzi et al., 2026) — none restricts its evidence base to comparative studies with a genuine antibiotic-monotherapy comparator, and none provides a pathogen-specific estimate for multidrug-resistant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. Using documented Boolean searches of PubMed/MEDLINE and ClinicalTrials.gov [EMBASE/Web of Science/Scopus pending institutional access], we systematically searched for the comparative evidence base for adjuvant phage therapy vs. antibiotic monotherapy in MDR *P. aeruginosa* and find [X comparative studies / that the evidence base remains too sparse to support meta-analytic pooling] — a gap that a newly registered pilot trial (NCT07698002) suggests the field is only now beginning to address. This has direct implications for how future comparative trials in this space should be designed."

This framing is honest about all findings from both search rounds (the temporal premise is wrong and the review count is even larger than initially found; the substantive gap is real and now more rigorously documented; a competing/adjacent trial is in the pipeline) and gives the strategist a workable, defensible starting point regardless of which of the five options above is chosen. **Round 3 refinement: per the frontier_map.md Round 3 update, the phrase "none restricts its evidence base to comparative studies with a genuine antibiotic-monotherapy comparator" should be tightened to "none combines a genuine antibiotic-monotherapy arm, a genuine phage+antibiotic-combination arm, and Pseudomonas-specific MDR/XDR classification in the same study" — Leitner et al. (2021) shows a real antibiotic-monotherapy arm has been tested against phage monotherapy, just not against a combination arm, and not in an MDR-Pseudomonas-specific population.**

## Round 2 Explicit Access-Gap Disclosure

**EMBASE, Web of Science, and Scopus were not searched in Round 1 or Round 2.** All Round 2 searching used only: (a) PubMed/MEDLINE via the public E-Utilities API (documented Boolean queries, exact strings and hit counts recorded in `annotated_bibliography.md`), (b) ClinicalTrials.gov structured API v2, and (c) indirect WebSearch-mediated spot-checks of PROSPERO and WHO ICTRP (both of which have JS/session-gated search interfaces that could not be queried directly by this agent). This means:
- Non-English-language comparative studies (better covered by EMBASE than PubMed) remain unchecked.
- Conference-abstract-only data (ECCMID, IDWeek, ASM Microbe — better indexed in EMBASE/Scopus than PubMed) remains unchecked.
- Full citation-graph "cited by" analysis for the three anchor reviews (El Haddad 2019, Uyttebroek 2022, Liu et al. 2025) via Web of Science/Scopus citation indices (more complete than what is achievable here) has not been done.
- Non-U.S./non-EU trial registries (ChiCTR, CTRI, ANZCTR) beyond what ClinicalTrials.gov itself indexes have not been directly checked.

**This gap requires the user's own institutional access to close and cannot be resolved by this agent.** Given the strength of triangulated evidence already assembled (documented PubMed Boolean queries, ClinicalTrials.gov structured search, three independent reviews — Fatima & Hynes 2025, Yan 2026, Terzi 2026 — all independently stating the same gap), this is now a residual-risk disclosure rather than a load-bearing uncertainty, but it should still be closed before a systematic review manuscript is submitted to a journal that will expect PRISMA-compliant multi-database search documentation.

## ROUND 3 ADDENDUM (2026-07-18) — Five New Study Leads from Liu et al. (2025) Supplementary Data

**Full per-study detail is in `annotated_bibliography.md`** ("Round 3: Five New Study Leads from Liu et al. (2025) Supplementary Data"). Summary of positioning-relevant implications:

1. **Contribution statement requires tightening, not abandonment.** The Leitner et al. (2021) finding (a genuine, already-published antibiotic-monotherapy RCT arm) narrows — but does not close — this project's central gap claim. See the revised Suggested Contribution Statement language above.
2. **Krakhotkin et al. (2025) is the closest structural precedent found in this entire search for the monotherapy-vs-combination stratification** (four arms: antibiotic-only, phage-only, phage+one antibiotic, phage+two antibiotics), conditional on confirming Pseudomonas-specific subgroup data in the full text. If that subgroup exists, this project should explicitly differentiate its MDR/XDR-*Pseudomonas*-specific stratification from Krakhotkin's general-MDR, urology-specific comparative design.
3. **Two source-table verification failures were identified in Liu et al. (2025)'s supplementary data** (Krakhotkin misclassified as RCT; Stanley misattributed to the wrong journal). This is a useful, citable data point for this project's own methodological rigor narrative ("independent verification of source characterizations, not reliance on secondary tabulation") and should be mentioned in the Methods section's description of the search/verification process.
4. **None of the five new leads changes the "0-1 genuine comparative studies for MDR-Pseudomonas-specific phage-vs-monotherapy" bottom line** — all require full-text retrieval to confirm Pseudomonas-specific, MDR/XDR-classified, arm-level data before they can be counted as eligible sources, and even if all four qualify, they would modestly enlarge (not resolve) the sparse evidence base already documented in Rounds 1-2.
