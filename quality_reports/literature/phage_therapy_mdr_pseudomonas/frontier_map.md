# Frontier Map — Adjuvant Phage Therapy vs. Antibiotic Monotherapy in MDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*

## What's Well-Established

- **Biological plausibility of phage-antibiotic synergy** is well-established at the mechanistic and preclinical level: phage-antibiotic combinations show enhanced killing over either agent alone in vitro and in murine models (e.g., Nature Communications 2025 VAP mouse model; multiple in vitro biofilm studies). This is not contested.
- **Single-arm/case-series clinical evidence is abundant and broadly positive.** Across ESKAPE pathogens generally and *P. aeruginosa* specifically, compassionate-use and personalized-therapy case series consistently report 70-90%+ "clinical improvement" or "eradication" rates (El Haddad 2019: 87% of studies showed efficacy; Pirnay 2024: n=100 real-world cases; Green 2023/PASA16: >80% favorable outcome). This evidence base is large but methodologically weak (no comparator, high risk of selection into compassionate-use for the most motivated/treatable cases, and inconsistent outcome definitions).
- **Safety profile is consistently favorable** across essentially all published human phage therapy studies, with low rates of serious adverse events attributable to phage administration.
- **Systematic review activity on phage therapy has been continuous, not dormant, since 2019.** At least six systematic reviews/meta-analyses of human phage therapy evidence have appeared 2022-2025 (Aranaga 2022; Uyttebroek 2022 Lancet ID; Ibrahim 2025; Liu et al. 2025 IJAA meta-analysis; plus a CF-specific 2023 review and a Pseudomonas-specific 2026 narrative review). **This is well-established and directly contradicts the project's founding premise** that no review has appeared since 2019. **Round 2 update (2026-07-18): a more rigorous, direct PubMed E-Utilities pass (see `annotated_bibliography.md`, "PRISMA-Grade Follow-Up Search Round 2") revised this count upward to eight-to-nine confirmed reviews, adding Terlizzi et al. (2026, Int J Infect Dis, CF-specific) and Terzi et al. (2026, Respiratory Medicine, MDR-respiratory-pathogen scoping review) to the list. The conclusion strengthens further; it does not change direction.**

## What's Contested / Unresolved

- **Whether phage-antibiotic combination causes better clinical outcomes than antibiotics alone**, in the causal-inference sense, is genuinely unresolved — not because the evidence points both ways, but because **almost no study design exists that could answer the question**. The one RCT with a comparator arm for *P. aeruginosa* specifically (PhagoBurn/Jault 2019) found the phage arm underperformed standard of care, which is inconsistent with the case-series-driven optimism and is rarely foregrounded in narrative reviews that otherwise report high "success rates."
- **Whether "success" in the case-series literature reflects a treatment effect or reflects confounding by indication / publication bias / outcome-reporting flexibility** (only successful cases described as "compassionate use," heterogeneous eradication definitions, no pre-registered outcome). This is a recurring, explicit critique in Fatima & Hynes (2025) and implicit in every case-series-based review.
- **Optimal comparator and outcome definitions for future comparative trials** — CF trials use placebo-on-background-antibiotics; PhagoBurn used standard wound care; there is no consensus "antibiotic monotherapy" comparator design that has actually been implemented for invasive/systemic MDR *Pseudomonas* infections. **Round 2 update: a newly registered pilot trial, NCT07698002 ("DePHEAT-PJI"), is the first identified registered trial to use a genuine antibiotics-alone control arm (vs. placebo or SOC-bundle) — see `positioning.md` for the implications of this for the design-gap framing. Round 3 update: this claim required a correction — see below.**

## What's Genuinely Open — The Gap This Project Targets

- **There is essentially no published human comparative (dual-arm) study evaluating adjuvant phage therapy vs. antibiotic monotherapy specifically for MDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* infections**, with mortality or microbiological eradication as outcomes. This gap is independently corroborated by three lines of evidence in this search:
  1. Direct search for comparative cohort/RCT designs turned up essentially one candidate (PhagoBurn) with a comparator that does not cleanly match "antibiotic monotherapy," plus three CF placebo-controlled RCTs with mismatched population/comparator/outcome.
  2. El Haddad (2019) itself explicitly notes that of 30 studies reviewed, only 9 used phage+antibiotic combination therapy, and none report a formal monotherapy comparator group — this gap pre-dates 2019 and has not visibly closed.
  3. Fatima & Hynes (2025) and Yan et al. (2026) — the two most recent Pseudomonas-focused reviews — both explicitly note that clinical (human) evidence connecting phage-antibiotic combination to comparative outcomes is essentially absent; the "comparative" evidence they can point to is preclinical/animal.
  4. **Round 2 update: a fourth independent corroborating source was identified — Terzi et al. (2026) scoping review of inhaled phage for MDR respiratory pathogens explicitly concludes "standardized protocols and controlled trials are needed." Round 2 also ran four documented PubMed Boolean queries (including one built directly from the librarian-critic's suggested negative-claim terms: matched cohort, propensity score, registry, case-control) plus two ClinicalTrials.gov structured API queries — none surfaced a qualifying completed comparative study. See `annotated_bibliography.md` for exact query strings and hit counts.**
- **No existing systematic review or meta-analysis restricts its design to comparative (within-study, dual-arm) evidence.** All identified reviews (El Haddad 2019, Aranaga 2022, Uyttebroek 2022, Ibrahim 2025, Liu et al. 2025, Terlizzi et al. 2026, Terzi et al. 2026) pool single-arm case series and heterogeneous designs together, generally reporting proportions ("X% clinical improvement") rather than a comparative effect size (RR/OR/HR vs. a true comparator). **This is a real methodological gap this project could fill — IF a design change is made (see positioning.md) to accommodate the sparse comparative evidence base**, e.g., by broadening pathogen scope, broadening comparator definition, or explicitly reframing as a "landscape/feasibility" paper rather than a meta-analysis with pooled effect sizes.
- **No Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews entry exists on phage therapy for any indication** as of this search — a genuine, still-open gap in the evidence-synthesis infrastructure for this literature, independent of the 2019 question.
- **Round 2 new finding — the design gap is starting to be addressed by others, on a 2-3 year horizon.** NCT07698002 ("DePHEAT-PJI"), a pilot feasibility RCT registered by the Ottawa Hospital Research Institute (not yet recruiting, estimated start July 2026), uses a genuine antibiotics-alone control arm for adjunctive phage therapy in *S. aureus*/*P. aeruginosa* periprosthetic joint infection. It is not MDR-restricted and will not report before 2027-2028, so it does not close the current evidence gap — but it is the first registered trial found in either search round with a PICO-matching comparator design, and should be tracked as a forward-looking scooping risk (see `positioning.md`).

## Implication for This Project's Design

The frontier confirms two things simultaneously:
1. The **contribution framing** ("first update since 2019") is factually wrong and must be revised — there have been several reviews since 2019, most notably a 2022 Lancet Infectious Diseases review and a 2025 IJAA meta-analysis. **Round 2 strengthens this further: at least eight-to-nine reviews now confirmed via direct PubMed querying, not six.**
2. The **substantive gap the researcher is actually pointing at is real** — no existing synthesis isolates comparative (dual-arm) evidence for the phage-vs-monotherapy question in MDR *Pseudomonas* — but the reason no one has done this synthesis is very likely that **the primary literature to support it does not yet exist in sufficient volume**, not that no one has thought of it. **Round 2 confirms this via a more rigorous, documented search methodology (PubMed E-Utilities Boolean queries + ClinicalTrials.gov structured API) and adds one important nuance: a registered pilot trial (DePHEAT-PJI) suggests the field is just beginning to design studies that would close this gap, which is itself evidence the gap is real and recognized.** This is the central finding for `/strategize` to grapple with before committing to a meta-analytic design.

## ROUND 3 UPDATE (2026-07-18) — Correction: A Genuine Antibiotic-Monotherapy Comparator Does Exist

**This section corrects a claim repeated in "What's Contested/Unresolved" and "What's Genuinely Open" above.** Round 3 screened five new study leads surfaced from Liu et al. (2025)'s supplementary RCT RoB2 table (full detail in `annotated_bibliography.md`, "Round 3" section). One finding materially updates this frontier map:

- **Leitner et al. (2021), Lancet Infectious Diseases** — a three-arm RCT (intravesical Pyophage vs. placebo vs. **open-label systemic antibiotics**, n=113, men undergoing TURP with UTI) — contains a **genuine antibiotic-monotherapy arm** (systemic antibiotics, no phage), which is cleaner than PhagoBurn's standard-of-care-bundle comparator and cleaner than the CF trials' placebo-on-background-antibiotics design. This had not been catalogued in Rounds 1-2. **The frontier map's prior framing — "there is no consensus antibiotic-monotherapy comparator design that has actually been implemented" — should be narrowed:** such a comparator design *has* been implemented, just not in an MDR-*Pseudomonas*-specific, phage+antibiotic **combination** trial. Leitner et al. tests phage-alone vs. antibiotic-alone vs. placebo — it does not test the combination arm this project's third stratification variable requires, and its population (UTI, mixed uropathogens, no MDR/XDR inclusion criterion) does not match this project's pathogen/resistance scope. Pseudomonas is confirmed among the enrolled pathogen panel but the Pseudomonas-specific proportion of the 113 randomized patients is not yet confirmed (full text needed).

**Net effect on the gap claim:** The core finding — **no study combines (a) MDR/XDR-*Pseudomonas*-specific population, (b) a genuine antibiotic-monotherapy arm, and (c) a phage+antibiotic combination arm, all in the same trial** — still holds. What changes is the more precise articulation of *which* piece of that three-part gap is genuinely unfilled: it is not "no antibiotic-monotherapy comparator has ever been tried against phage" (false — Leitner 2021 did this, just without a combination arm and without MDR/Pseudomonas specificity); it is "no trial or comparative study combines a real antibiotic-monotherapy arm, a real phage+antibiotic-combination arm, and Pseudomonas-specific MDR/XDR classification, in the same study." This is a **narrower, more defensible, and more accurate gap statement** than the pre-Round-3 framing, and should replace the broader claim in the manuscript's Introduction/Discussion once drafted.

**Also newly catalogued in Round 3** (see `annotated_bibliography.md` for full detail): Krakhotkin et al. (2025) — an observational (not randomized, contra Liu et al.'s table) four-arm comparative study in women with MDR-cystitis, structurally the closest match anywhere in this search to the monotherapy-vs-combination-vs-phage-alone stratification, pending confirmation of Pseudomonas-specific subgroup data; Karn et al. (2024) and Stanley et al. (2025) — two more placebo-controlled, phage-add-on-to-background-antibiotics RCTs (wound and CF/inhaled route respectively), incremental additions to an already-identified design class; and Rhoads et al. (2009) — a low-relevance, saline-controlled wound safety trial that corrects a bibliographic gap (previously PMID-referenced in Round 2 but never fully catalogued) rather than adding new evidence.

**None of the four Round-3 comparative/RCT leads (Krakhotkin, Leitner, Karn, Stanley) is yet confirmed to report a Pseudomonas-specific, MDR/XDR-classified, arm-level outcome count** — all require full-text retrieval before they can be counted toward this project's proportion-extraction feasibility. This reinforces the frontier map's central conclusion: the literature is broad at the "phage therapy RCT" level but still narrow at the "Pseudomonas-specific, MDR-classified, stratified-by-modality" level this project's design requires.
