## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — Answers to the Two Critical Questions

### 1. The "2019 anchor review" — FOUND, BUT THE RESEARCHER'S PREMISE IS FALSE

**The best match for "the 2019 review":**
> El Haddad L, Harb CP, Gebara MA, Stibich MA, Chemaly RF. **"A Systematic and Critical Review of Bacteriophage Therapy Against Multidrug-resistant ESKAPE Organisms in Humans."** *Clinical Infectious Diseases* 2019;69(1):167–178. doi:10.1093/cid/ciy947. (Published online Nov 3, 2018; print issue July 2019.)

This is a strong match: published in a top target journal (CID), explicitly framed as a "systematic and critical review," covers ESKAPE pathogens with *P. aeruginosa* as the single largest represented organism (30% of the 30 included studies), and is well-cited as *the* reference synthesis of human phage therapy evidence from that era.

**However — this is NOT the most recent systematic review, and the "nothing has updated it since 2019" premise does not hold.** At minimum six systematic reviews/meta-analyses of human phage therapy have been published since El Haddad et al. (2019):

| Year | Review | Journal | Notes |

| 2022 | Aranaga et al., "Phage Therapy in the Era of Multidrug Resistance in Bacteria: A Systematic Review" | Int J Mol Sci | 27 studies, 165 patients |
| 2022 | **Uyttebroek et al.**, "Safety and efficacy of phage therapy in difficult-to-treat infections: a systematic review" | **Lancet Infectious Diseases** | Search through Aug 14, 2021. High-impact, large multi-author consortium (incl. Pirnay, Sciensano/Belgian phage group). This is the most likely candidate for "the real update" the researcher has not seen. |
| 2022 | (Lancet Microbe) "Efficacy of phage therapy in preclinical models of bacterial infection: a systematic review and meta-analysis" | Lancet Microbe | Different scope — animal/preclinical models only, not human |
| 2023 | CF-specific systematic review, phage therapy in *S. aureus* and *P. aeruginosa* in cystic fibrosis | Paediatric Respiratory Reviews (confirmed PMID 37598024 in Round 2 — see below) | Literature to March 2023 |

This is the single most direct and recent scooping-risk paper — an actual meta-analysis, in a strong ID journal, current as of a few months before this search. **Fully verified in Round 2 (PMID 40633848) — see below.** |
| 2026 | Yan et al., "Multidrug-resistant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* infections: current status, challenges, and prospects of phage therapy" | Frontiers in Microbiology | Narrative (not systematic), Pseudomonas-specific, references to 2025 |

**Verdict: found something different than expected.** El Haddad (2019, CID) is very likely the specific paper the researcher remembers, but it is **not the last synthesis** — at least two subsequent reviews (Uyttebroek 2022 in Lancet ID; Liu et al. 2025 in IJAA) are more recent, broader or comparably rigorous, and one (Liu et al. 2025) is an actual meta-analysis published only ~2 months before this search. **The 2019-present search cutoff and the "first update since 2019" contribution framing do NOT hold as stated and must be revised** (see `positioning.md`).

**Round 2 update (2026-07-18, PubMed API pass): this count is revised UPWARD, not downward — see the Round 2 section below. At least two further reviews/scoping reviews (Terlizzi et al. 2026; Terzi et al. 2026) were identified via direct PubMed querying, bringing the confirmed total to at least eight review-type syntheses since 2019.**

No standalone **Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews** entry on phage therapy (for any pathogen) was found — this remains a genuine gap in the evidence-synthesis literature, distinct from the "since 2019" question.

### 2. Comparative-study count (phage+antibiotic vs. antibiotic-monotherapy, same study, MDR *P. aeruginosa*) — CRITICAL FEASIBILITY FAILURE

**Count of qualifying comparative studies found (2019–present, human, MDR *P. aeruginosa*, both arms in the same study): approximately ZERO to ONE**, and the one candidate does not cleanly satisfy the PICO comparator.

- **Jault et al. 2019 ("PhagoBurn"), Lancet Infectious Diseases** — an open-label, randomized, standard-of-care-controlled Phase 1/2 trial (NCT02116010) of a phage cocktail vs. standard of care (silver sulfadiazine dressing, with usual antibiotics per clinical practice in both arms) for *P. aeruginosa*-infected burn wounds. This is the **only registered RCT with a genuine within-study comparator arm** identified for *P. aeruginosa* specifically. It does **not** cleanly match the PICO comparator ("antibiotic monotherapy") — the comparator is standard wound care, not a defined antibiotic-only arm — and, notably, the phage arm **underperformed** the comparator (median 144h vs. 47h to reach the primary endpoint). Proximity 2, with this caveat flagged explicitly.
- Three cystic fibrosis RCTs (Armata AP-PA02/"SWARM-P.a.", Phase 1b/2a; BX004-A, Phase 1b/2a; TP-102, Phase 1/2) are placebo-controlled trials in which **both** arms receive background antibiotic therapy — i.e., phage-as-add-on vs. placebo-as-add-on. These are structurally adjacent to the PICO design (adjuvant intervention layered on standard antibiotics) but: (a) comparator is placebo, not defined "antibiotic monotherapy"; (b) population is chronic CF colonization, not necessarily meeting a strict MDR/invasive-infection definition; (c) primary outcomes are bacterial density/pharmacokinetics/safety, not mortality or classically-defined microbiological eradication. Proximity 2–3. **All three now have confirmed peer-reviewed primary publications as of the Round 2 PubMed pass — see below; this upgrades their evidentiary status from "conference abstract/press release" to "verified peer-reviewed RCT," though it does not change their PICO-eligibility.** **Round 3 update: a fourth CF placebo-controlled RCT of the same design class, Stanley et al. (2025)/CYPHY (YPT-01), is added below — see Round 3 section.**
- **No retrospective or prospective cohort study was found** that reports both a phage+antibiotic arm and an antibiotic-monotherapy arm for MDR *P. aeruginosa* within the same study. All other human evidence located (Pirnay et al. 2024, Petrovic-Fabijan et al. 2020, Green et al. 2023/PASA16, individual case reports) is **single-arm** (all patients received phage; no monotherapy comparator group).
- Checking backward through El Haddad (2019)'s own reference list and forward citation chains did not surface additional pre-2019 comparative studies for *Pseudomonas* specifically; pre-2019 comparative phage RCTs that do exist (e.g., Wright et al. 2009, chronic otitis, phage vs. placebo) are not MDR-*Pseudomonas*-invasive-infection studies and use placebo, not antibiotic-monotherapy, as comparator. **Round 3 update: one materially important exception was found — Leitner et al. (2021), Lancet Infectious Diseases, has a genuine three-arm design including a real "systemic antibiotics" monotherapy arm (not just placebo/SOC-bundle). See Round 3 section below — this is the closest genuine antibiotic-monotherapy comparator found in either search round, though population is UTI (mixed pathogens, not MDR-restricted), not invasive MDR *Pseudomonas* infection.** **Round 4 update: exhaustively re-investigated — see the dedicated Round 4 section below. Verdict unchanged: this remains the closest genuine antibiotic-monotherapy arm, but the Pseudomonas-specific subgroup remains unresolved due to a disclosed full-text access limitation, not a negative finding.**

**This count is far below the ~5–10 study feasibility threshold specified in `quality_reports/decisions/discovery_phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas.md`.** Per that decision record's own invalidation clause, **the comparative-studies-only design as currently scoped (MDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* specifically) is not viable for a meta-analysis.** See `frontier_map.md` and `positioning.md` for implications and options.

**Round 2 update (2026-07-18): this conclusion HOLDS after a more rigorous, documented PubMed E-utilities + ClinicalTrials.gov API search — see the full Round 2 section below for exact queries, hit counts, and one important new registered-but-not-yet-recruiting trial (NCT07698002, "DePHEAT-PJI") that changes the forward-looking framing without changing the current evidence count.**

**Note: the research spec has since (2026-07-18) been revised to a meta-analysis of proportions design** (see `quality_reports/research_spec_phage_therapy_mdr_pseudomonas.md`), under which both single-arm and comparative studies are eligible — this resolves the feasibility failure described above for the *original* comparative-only design. The Round 3 screen below evaluates the five new study leads against the **current (proportions) design**, not the original comparative-only design.

## Papers by Proximity Score

### Proximity 2 — Closely related (comparative study design, but comparator/population/pathogen scope diverges from strict PICO)

#### Jault et al. (2019) - PhagoBurn: RCT of Phage Cocktail vs. Standard of Care for *P. aeruginosa* Burn Wound Infections
- **Journal:** Lancet Infectious Diseases
- **Study design:** Randomized, controlled, open-label, standard-of-care-controlled, Phase 1/2 trial (NCT02116010)
- **Proximity:** 2
- **Main contribution:** Only identified RCT with a genuine within-study comparator arm for phage therapy against *P. aeruginosa* specifically. Multi-center (9 burn centers), n=27 (13 phage, 14 SOC).
- **Population:** *P. aeruginosa*-infected burn wounds; MDR status not a strict enrollment criterion (broader "infected burn wound" population)
- **Key finding:** Primary endpoint (sustained 2-quadrant reduction in bacterial burden) reached slower in the phage arm (median 144h, 95% CI 48-NR) than standard of care (median 47h, 23-122) — i.e., **phage therapy performed worse**, not better, than the comparator in this trial. Attributed partly to declining phage titer in the therapeutic cocktail over the trial period.
- **Relevance:** The single clearest comparative RCT in this space; must be included and discussed even though it does not support the hypothesized direction of effect, and its comparator (SOC, not "antibiotic monotherapy" per se) needs explicit handling in eligibility criteria.

#### Armata Pharmaceuticals — SWARM-P.a. Trial (AP-PA02) — Phase 1b/2a RCT, Inhaled Phage vs. Placebo in CF *P. aeruginosa*
- **Journal:** Topline results via press release/conference (BioSpace, PRNewswire) at time of Round 1 search; **Round 2 update: trial confirmed COMPLETED (Dec 14, 2022) with structured results POSTED on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04596319, hasResults=true) — verified via ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 in Round 2. No peer-reviewed journal primary-results publication was located via PubMed in either round; results exist only in the CT.gov structured results database.**
- **Study design:** Multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single/multiple ascending dose trial; NCT04596319 (confirmed)
- **Proximity:** 2
- **Main contribution:** Both arms continued background CF antibiotic therapy; add-on comparison of inhaled phage vs. placebo. n=29 actual (confirmed via CT.gov).
- **Population:** Cystic fibrosis patients with chronic pulmonary *P. aeruginosa* infection (not necessarily "MDR" by strict definition; chronic colonization, not acute MDR infection)
- **Key finding:** Primary outcome = incidence/severity of treatment-emergent adverse events; highest-dose cohort (Cohort 4) had 5/10 subjects with TEAEs (one serious event, small intestinal obstruction, in the AP-PA02 arm) vs. 2/3 (SAD placebo) and 4/5 (MAD placebo). Structured efficacy (bacterial density) comparison between arms not reported in the CT.gov results summary fields reviewed.
- **Relevance:** Structurally close to PICO (adjuvant vs. background antibiotics) but wrong population/outcome focus for the review as scoped; the "unverified, press-release-only" flag from Round 1 is now resolved — this is a completed, registered trial with posted structured results, just not (yet) a peer-reviewed journal article.

#### BX004-A / Phase 1b/2a Nebulized Phage Trial in CF *P. aeruginosa*
- **Journal:** **Round 2 update: FULL PEER-REVIEWED PUBLICATION CONFIRMED — Weiner et al., "Phage therapy with nebulized cocktail BX004-A for chronic Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections in cystic fibrosis: a randomized first-in-human trial," *Nature Communications* 16:5579 (July 1, 2025), doi:10.1038/s41467-025-60598-4, PMID 40593506. An Author Correction was subsequently published (PMID 41760669, Feb 2026) — check the correction before citing specific numbers.**
- **Study design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter, Phase 1/2, first-in-human trial. n=9 adult CF patients (arm-level split not specified in abstract).
- **Proximity:** 2
- **Main contribution:** Confirms efficient phage delivery to the lower respiratory tract; primary endpoints of safety/tolerability were met. Exploratory endpoints (pharmacokinetics, *P. aeruginosa* sputum density) showed a "potential reduction" in the phage arm, but the authors explicitly caution that the small sample size limits efficacy conclusions.
- **Relevance:** Same structural caveats as before (comparator is placebo, not antibiotic monotherapy; CF chronic-colonization population, not necessarily MDR) — but this fully resolves the Round 1 "UNVERIFIED, conference-abstract-only" flag. Should be cited via the Nature Communications DOI, not the conference-abstract secondary source.

#### TP-102 — Randomized, Double-Blind Trial of Phage Cocktail in Diabetic Foot Ulcers (mixed organisms incl. *P. aeruginosa*)
- **Journal:** **Round 2 update: FULL PEER-REVIEWED PUBLICATION CONFIRMED — Nir-Paz et al., "Randomized double-blind study on safety and tolerability of TP-102 phage cocktail in patients with infected and non-infected diabetic foot ulcers," *Med* (Cell Press) 6(5):100565 (May 9, 2025), doi:10.1016/j.medj.2024.11.018, PMID 39740667.**
- **Study design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (both arms on standard of care). n=19 total (13 TP-102+SOC, ~6 placebo+SOC, per abstract-reported proportions).
- **Main contribution:** Phage cocktail (5 phages, targets *S. aureus*, *P. aeruginosa*, *A. baumannii*) vs. placebo, both on standard wound care, in infected/non-infected diabetic foot ulcers.
- **Key finding:** No adverse events in the treatment group; microbiological reduction reported as 80% (TP-102+SOC) vs. 50% (placebo+SOC); wound closure (≥50%) in 71.4% vs. 33.3%; (≥75%) in 28.6% vs. 0%. **No Pseudomonas-specific or MDR-specific subgroup breakdown was reported in the abstract** — pathogens are pooled across the three target organisms.
- **Relevance:** Same structural caveats as the CF trials — mixed pathogen population, placebo comparator (on top of SOC), no Pseudomonas-specific or MDR-specific stratification reported. Round 1's "UNVERIFIED" flag is now resolved with full bibliographic detail.

### Proximity 3 — Related (phage therapy for *Pseudomonas*, single-arm or different outcome focus)

#### Pirnay et al. (2024) - Personalized Bacteriophage Therapy Outcomes for 100 Consecutive Cases
- **Journal:** Nature Microbiology, 9(6), 1434–1453
- **Study design:** Multicentre, multinational, retrospective **observational** (single-arm) case series
- **Proximity:** 3
- **Main contribution:** Largest published dataset of real-world personalized phage therapy (35 hospitals, 29 cities, 12 countries, 2008–2022). *P. aeruginosa* was the most common target pathogen (49/100 patients).
- **Population:** Mixed pathogens; MDR *Pseudomonas* well represented but no monotherapy comparator arm
- **Key finding:** Regression analysis found bacterial clearance ~70% less probable when phages were not combined with antibiotics — supports biological plausibility of phage-antibiotic synergy, but this is a within-phage-recipients comparison (antibiotic-combined vs. phage-alone within the treated group), not a phage-vs-monotherapy comparison.
- **Relevance:** Key single-arm dataset; useful for mechanism/plausibility discussion and for context on how large this literature actually is outside the comparative-study restriction. Not eligible under the project's comparative-only design.

#### Petrovic Fabijan et al. (2020) - Safety of Bacteriophage Therapy in Severe *Staphylococcus aureus* Infection
- **Journal:** Nature Microbiology (referenced in search results; exact title/details not independently re-verified in this pass — cross-check before citing — marked UNVERIFIED pending direct confirmation)
- **Study design:** Case series (13 patients)
- **Proximity:** 3 (background — primarily *S. aureus*, included for completeness given frequent co-citation with Pseudomonas phage literature)
- **Relevance:** Frequently cited alongside Pseudomonas phage case series in reviews; useful background, not core.

#### Green et al. (2023) - Refractory *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* Infections Treated with Phage PASA16: A Compassionate Use Case Series
- **Journal:** Med (Cell Press)
- **Study design:** Single-arm compassionate-use case series
- **Proximity:** 3
- **Main contribution:** One of the larger single-phage (PASA16) compassionate-use case series specific to *P. aeruginosa*.
- **Population:** Refractory *P. aeruginosa* infections (severity/refractoriness consistent with salvage-therapy selection bias)
- **Key finding:** Favorable outcome in >80% of treated patients; minimal side effects reported.
- **Relevance:** Illustrates the "single-arm, refractory/salvage population" pattern that motivated the project's decision to exclude such studies as primary evidence (see decision record) — good example to cite when justifying the comparative-only design in the manuscript.

#### CF-specific systematic review (2023) - Bacteriophage Therapy for *S. aureus* and *P. aeruginosa* in Cystic Fibrosis
- **Journal:** **Round 2 update: CONFIRMED via PubMed — this is Paediatric Respiratory Reviews (Dec 2023), PMID 37598024, "A systematic review on the use of bacteriophage in treating Staphylococcus aureus and Pseudomonas aeruginosa infections in cystic fibrosis." PROSPERO registration CRD42020207622 (confirmed via web search of the PROSPERO record, since direct PROSPERO API/search-form querying was blocked — see Round 2 Limitations).**
- **Study design:** Systematic review (narrative), literature to March 2023
- **Proximity:** 3
- **Relevance:** Anatomic/population-specific systematic review confirming that even narrower syntheses since 2019 exist; none use a comparative-only design.

#### Nature Communications (2025) - Adjunctive Phage Therapy Improves Antibiotic Treatment of Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia with *P. aeruginosa*
- **Journal:** Nature Communications, 16:4500
- **Study design:** **Preclinical murine model** (C57BL/6J mice, VAP model), NOT a human study — includes a true 2x2 design (meropenem alone, phage alone, combination, PBS control)
- **Proximity:** 3 (background/mechanistic — flagged as NOT eligible for the human-subjects meta-analysis, but directly relevant to the mechanistic rationale and to framing why an adjuvant-effect hypothesis is biologically motivated)
- **Key finding:** Combination treatment produced faster clinical improvement and reduced lung epithelial damage vs. either monotherapy in the mouse model.
- **Relevance:** Best-designed "comparative" evidence for the adjuvant hypothesis found anywhere in this search — but it is preclinical, not human. Important for the Introduction/mechanism section, not the evidence table.

### Proximity 4 — Background (systematic reviews/meta-analyses of phage therapy — mechanism, resistance biology, general AMR context; also candidates for the "2019 anchor")

#### El Haddad et al. (2019) - A Systematic and Critical Review of Bacteriophage Therapy Against Multidrug-resistant ESKAPE Organisms in Humans
- **Journal:** Clinical Infectious Diseases, 69(1):167–178
- **Study design:** Systematic review (narrative, not meta-analytic)
- **Proximity:** 4 (background/anchor — sets the project's originally-assumed search cutoff)
- **Main contribution:** 30 studies, 1,152+ patients, ESKAPE pathogens; *P. aeruginosa* most represented (30%). Concluded 87% of studies showed phage efficacy; safety favorable in 67%.
- **Population:** Mixed ESKAPE pathogens, overwhelmingly single-arm case reports/series; only 9/30 studies used phage+antibiotic combination (322 patients), and none of the 30 had a formal monotherapy comparator arm reported in the review.
- **Relevance:** This is the paper the researcher most likely remembers as "the 2019 review." Confirmed AS a real, well-matched 2019 CID systematic review — but its "no comparative studies" finding at the time is itself evidence that the comparative-study gap identified by this project pre-dates 2019 and has not obviously closed since.

#### Uyttebroek et al. (2022) - Safety and Efficacy of Phage Therapy in Difficult-to-Treat Infections: A Systematic Review
- **Journal:** Lancet Infectious Diseases, 22(8):e208–e220
- **Study design:** Systematic review (narrative), literature Jan 1, 2000 – Aug 14, 2021
- **Proximity:** 4 — **but flagged as the most important "premise-breaking" finding of this search.**
- **Main contribution:** Large multi-author (Belgian/European phage consortium) synthesis in one of the highest-impact ID journals; directly supersedes El Haddad (2019) in recency and arguably in rigor/scope.
- **Relevance:** This is very likely the review the researcher's "no update since 2019" claim should be checked against. Must be cited explicitly in the paper's introduction and the search-cutoff must be revised forward from this review's endpoint (Aug 2021) rather than 2019.

#### Aranaga et al. (2022) - Phage Therapy in the Era of Multidrug Resistance in Bacteria: A Systematic Review
- **Journal:** International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 23:4577
- **Study design:** Systematic review, 27 studies / 165 patients
- **Proximity:** 4
- **Relevance:** Another confirmed post-2019 systematic review; reinforces that "no review since 2019" is false.

#### Lancet Microbe (2022) - Efficacy of Phage Therapy in Preclinical Models of Bacterial Infection: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- **Journal:** Lancet Microbe
- **Study design:** Systematic review + meta-analysis, but of **preclinical (animal) models only** — different scope from the human-subjects question here
- **Proximity:** 4

#### Liu, Thong, Moreira, Yeo, Zhong, Chong et al. (2025) - Clinical Application of Customized and Non-Customized Bacteriophage Therapy in Patients with Refractory/Resistant Bacterial Infections: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
- **Journal:** International Journal of Antimicrobial Agents, 66(4):107570
- **Study design:** Systematic review + **individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis**; 16,226 records screened (1929–May 2025), 130 articles included
- **Proximity:** 4 — **the single highest scooping-risk paper identified in this search.**
- **Main contribution:** Broad, cross-pathogen IPD meta-analysis of phage therapy outcomes (clinical improvement, bacterial eradication), comparing customized vs. non-customized phage approaches (559 non-customized, 451 customized-selection, 105 customized-production patients per ScienceDirect abstract). - **Relevance:** This is the most recent, most methodologically ambitious phage therapy meta-analysis found, published within months of this search. **Round 2 update: FULLY VERIFIED via PubMed esummary — PMID 40633848, full 9-author list confirmed (Liu Y, Thong S, Moreira W, Yeo JH, Zhong Y, Chong ZS, Wong THN, Chung SJ, Kwa ALH), journal = International Journal of Antimicrobial Agents, Vol 66, Issue 4, p. 107570, pub date October 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.ijantimicag.2025.107570, publication types Journal Article / Systematic Review / Meta-Analysis. This resolves the librarian-critic's top-priority verification request (score 84/100 review, deduction item 1) — the paper is now confirmed to exist with complete bibliographic detail, not just an abstract-level secondary summary.** It does **not** appear to use the comparative-only design this project proposes, nor does it isolate MDR *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* as a dedicated subgroup with a monotherapy comparator (based on available abstract/secondary sources — full-text access remains blocked; **recommend the strategist/writer obtain full text before finalizing the contribution statement**). Differentiation from this paper is now central to the project's positioning (see `positioning.md`). **Round 3 note: this is also the paper whose supplementary RCT RoB2 table the orchestrator read independently, surfacing the five new study leads screened below — this project's own evidence base overlaps materially with Liu et al.'s underlying source pool, reinforcing that this is the single highest-priority scooping risk.**

## Limitations of This Search (Round 1)

- No direct institutional access to EMBASE, Web of Science, or Scopus was available in this environment; searches were conducted via WebSearch/WebFetch against PubMed-indexed abstracts, journal websites, and secondary aggregators. **A defensible PRISMA-compliant search (per domain-profile.md) will require the librarian or research team to re-run structured searches directly in PubMed/MEDLINE, EMBASE, and Cochrane CENTRAL with full Boolean/MeSH strategies** — this report should be treated as a scoping/feasibility pass, not the final systematic search.
- Several full-text articles were blocked by paywalls/authentication (ScienceDirect, Springer, Nature) — abstracts and secondary summaries were used where full text was inaccessible; entries relying on secondary summaries are flagged `UNVERIFIED` in the bibliography and should be independently confirmed before the systematic review's formal screening stage.
- ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP were searched via web search rather than the structured registry APIs; a formal registry search should be re-run during `/discover data` or the PRISMA search execution stage.

### A. PubMed E-Utilities — Exact Queries Run and Hit Counts

All queries run via `https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/esearch.fcgi` (public, no auth), `esummary.fcgi`, and `efetch.fcgi`. Full query strings and raw hit counts recorded below for auditability.

**Query 1 — Broad comparative-design query (Pseudomonas + phage + antibiotic + comparative-design MeSH/keyword filters):**
```
("Pseudomonas aeruginosa"[MeSH] OR "Pseudomonas aeruginosa"[tiab]) AND ("phage therapy"[tiab]
OR "bacteriophage therapy"[tiab] OR "Bacteriophages"[MeSH]) AND ("antibiotic"[tiab] OR
"antimicrobial"[tiab]) AND ("comparative study"[pt] OR "Cohort Studies"[MeSH] OR
"Retrospective Studies"[MeSH] OR "Case-Control Studies"[MeSH] OR "matched"[tiab] OR
"propensity"[tiab] OR "controls"[tiab])
```

**Hit count: 44.** This confirms that antibiotic-only treatment-outcome cohorts for MDR/XDR *P. aeruginosa* are **abundant** in the general infectious-disease literature — this is a fundamentally different (and far better populated) evidence base than the phage-therapy-comparative literature searched in Rounds 1–3. Four representative studies were directly verified via PubMed `esummary`:

- **Almangour et al. (2025), Pharmaceuticals (Basel), PMID 39861170.** Multicenter retrospective cohort, MDR *P. aeruginosa*, n=203 (89 ceftazidime-avibactam, 114 colistin). Clinical cure: 67.4% (CAZ-AVI) vs. 50% (colistin). In-hospital mortality: 40% vs. 49.1%.
- **Alsaeed et al. (2024), Cureus, PMID 39741620.** Single-center retrospective cohort, MDR *P. aeruginosa*, Dammam Medical Complex, three antibiotic regimens: ceftazidime-avibactam (cure 72%, mortality 24%), ceftolozane-tazobactam (cure 59%, mortality 32%), meropenem+colistin (cure 48%, mortality 38%).
- **Maraolo et al. (2026), Antibiotics (Basel), PMID 41594071.** Retrospective cohort of difficult-to-treat resistant (DTR) *P. aeruginosa* infections; antibiotic-only treatment; mortality predictors (full outcome figures not extracted in this pass — abstract-level identification only).
- **Corcione et al. (2025), PLoS One, PMID 40388472.** Large Italian multicenter retrospective cohort of *P. aeruginosa* bloodstream infections in internal medicine wards (not confirmed MDR-restricted at the whole-cohort level; lower priority, flagged for full-text MDR-subgroup confirmation).

**These are flagged explicitly as EXTERNAL BENCHMARK, NOT HEAD-TO-HEAD.** None of the four contains a bacteriophage arm of any kind — they compare different antibiotic regimens against each other, not phage-antibiotic combination against antibiotic monotherapy. They cannot be pooled into this project's comparative or proportion-meta-analysis strata as currently designed (which require either a within-study phage arm, per the original comparative design, or at minimum a phage-treated population, per the current proportions design). Their value is strictly as **contextual/background evidence** for the manuscript's Discussion or Introduction — e.g., establishing that typical clinical-cure rates for antibiotic-only salvage regimens in MDR/XDR *P. aeruginosa* range roughly 48–72% across recent multicenter cohorts, which can frame why an adjuvant-phage-therapy hypothesis is clinically motivated, without being usable as a formal comparator arm for this project's own pooled estimates. All four have been added to `references.bib`, explicitly marked with this caveat in their notes fields.

### C. Re-check of existing bibliography for an overlooked antibiotic-only comparator arm

Every comparative/cohort study already catalogued in Rounds 1–3 was re-reviewed specifically for an unnoticed antibiotic-monotherapy arm:

- **PhagoBurn (Jault et al. 2019):** comparator is standard-of-care wound dressing (silver sulfadiazine) with usual antibiotics per clinical practice in both arms — not a defined antibiotic-monotherapy arm. No change.
- **The four CF placebo-controlled RCTs (Armata/AP-PA02, BX004-A, TP-102, Stanley/CYPHY):** all four have background antibiotic therapy in **both** arms (phage-add-on vs. placebo-add-on) — there is no arm receiving antibiotics with no phage exposure attempted; structurally these can at most supply a "phage+antibiotic combination vs. antibiotic+placebo (antibiotic-monotherapy-equivalent)" contrast, not a clean monotherapy arm, and none is confirmed Pseudomonas-MDR-specific at the subgroup level. No change.
- **Karn et al. (2024):** same structure as the CF trials — background antibiotics in both arms, so the placebo arm is an antibiotic-monotherapy-equivalent arm structurally, but Pseudomonas-specificity remains unconfirmed pending full text. No change from Round 3's "conditionally eligible" status.
- **Rhoads et al. (2009):** comparator is saline, with no antibiotic in either arm — confirmed (again) to have no antibiotic axis at all. No change.
- **Krakhotkin et al. - **Leitner et al. (2021):** the systemic-antibiotics arm remains the only confirmed genuine antibiotic-monotherapy (no phage, no placebo) arm anywhere in this literature for a design that also includes phage. Status: conditionally eligible, blocked on access (see Section A above).

### D. Round 4 Verdict

**Is there ANY usable antibiotic-monotherapy data point for *P. aeruginosa*, within a study that also includes a phage arm?** As of this round: **no confirmed usable data point exists.** Krakhotkin is definitively excluded (zero *P. aeruginosa* cases). Leitner remains the sole structurally clean candidate (a genuine "antibiotics alone" arm, no placebo/SOC-bundle confound) but its Pseudomonas-specific subgroup could not be resolved through any accessible route in this pass — it is **blocked on full-text access**, not confirmed either eligible or ineligible. No new candidate (RCT, cohort, or case series with an internal antibiotic-only comparator alongside a phage arm) was found anywhere in this round's searches.

**Practical implication for the strategy memo's decision rule:** the monotherapy stratum should be reported **as insufficient evidence** in the current draft of the manuscript, consistent with the strategy memo's own invalidation clause — but this should be framed explicitly as "zero *confirmed* eligible studies, one study (Leitner 2021) blocked on access rather than confirmed ineligible," not as "zero studies exist in principle." If the research team can obtain Leitner's full text (institutional access, interlibrary loan, or direct author correspondence), this is the single highest-value next step for potentially resolving the monotherapy stratum before finalizing the "insufficient evidence" framing in the manuscript. The four external-benchmark antibiotic-only cohort studies identified in Section B (Almangour 2025, Alsaeed 2024, Maraolo 2026, Corcione 2025) are recommended for citation in the Discussion/Introduction as contextual background on typical antibiotic-monotherapy outcome rates in MDR/XDR *P. aeruginosa* — they are not a substitute for a within-study comparator and should never be presented as such.
