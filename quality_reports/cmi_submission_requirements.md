# CMI submission requirements — verified against the journal's own guide

**Journal:** *Clinical Microbiology and Infection* (CMI), official publication of
ESCMID (Elsevier).
**Source:** `clinicalmicrobiologyandinfection.com/content/authorinfo`, read
2026-08-03. Every figure below is quoted from that page, not recalled.
**Submission portal:** `mc.manuscriptcentral.com/cmi`

---

## The two numbers that govern everything

| Requirement | CMI | This manuscript | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Systematic Review main text** | **3,500 words** | **20,639** | must cut **83%** |
| **Structured abstract** | **300 words max** | 140, unstructured | rewrite and expand |

CMI is stricter than the 3,500–4,500 band assumed in the targeting memo: it sits
at the bottom of it. For comparison, CMI caps *Original Articles* at 2,500 words —
so a Systematic Review is already the most generous research format the journal
offers.

## The required abstract structure

CMI prescribes the sections verbatim for **Systematic Reviews of interventions**,
which is what this is:

> Background; Objectives; Methods: Data sources, Study eligibility criteria,
> Participants, Interventions, Assessment of risk of bias, Methods of data
> synthesis; Results; Conclusions.

Two consequences for this manuscript. **"Interventions" is a required abstract
field**, and the review currently does not characterise the intervention at all —
no product, dose, duration, or personalisation status — a limitation already
conceded in Methods. Something honest has to go in that slot. And **"Assessment
of risk of bias" is required**, so Murad's four domains and the GRADE floor
belong in the abstract, not only in the body.

## Reporting guidelines

> PRISMA for systematic reviews and meta-analysis […] The appropriate checklist
> should be submitted at the time of the article submission.

The **completed PRISMA 2020 checklist is a submission artefact**, not a
formatting nicety. It will be read against the manuscript, and its item 7 (full
search strategy per source, with all filters and limits) is the one this review
spent this session making answerable. Item 24a asks for the registration number.

CMI also points to its own editorial guidance on living systematic reviews and on
observational-study standards, which is worth reading before framing — a review
of an evidence base that is still accumulating case reports is a natural
candidate for the living-review format.

## What has to happen, in order

**1. Cut 20,639 → 3,500.** This is the whole job. Suggested allocation:

| Section | Target |
|---|---|
| Introduction | 350 |
| Methods | 700 |
| Results | 1,500 |
| Discussion | 900 |
| *(slack)* | 50 |

Everything else moves to supplementary material, which CMI accepts: the search
architecture and per-database counts, the stage-1 screening rules and their three
rejected alternatives, the cross-model comparison, the falsification analyses,
the document-corpus provenance audit, the full GRADE profile, the claim-source
map.

**2. Lead with the verification findings, not the pooled proportions.** At 1,500
words of Results there is room for perhaps three findings. They should be: the
classification collapse (17 studies → 2 studies, 3 patients under verified
labels), the eradication mixture artefact (τ² 1.995 → 0.000 on the site split),
and the reporting-completeness triad (resistance panels, in-vitro activity 3/40,
DTR non-derivability). The pooled proportions become context for those, reported
once with their GRADE rating attached.

**3. Write the structured abstract to CMI's exact section list**, including an
honest "Interventions" line.

**4. Complete the PRISMA 2020 checklist** and resolve the two items that no
amount of writing can fix: **PROSPERO registration** and **dual independent
screening**. CMI's reviewers will look for both.

## Why CMI over JAC

Both are Q1 and both publish systematic reviews. The split is audience. This
paper's two headline results — that Magiorakos labels do not survive independent
verification, and that microbiological eradication is not one construct across
infection sites — are findings about **microbiological measurement**, and CMI's
readership assigns those labels professionally. JAC's centre of gravity is
antimicrobial therapy and stewardship, where the same findings read as
methodological background rather than as the point.

If CMI declines, JAC is the natural second submission with the framing largely
unchanged. IJAA remains the strategic third, with the Liu 2025 consideration
recorded in the targeting memo.
