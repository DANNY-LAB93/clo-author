## Structured abstract (English) — 256 palabras

**Introduction.** Several recent syntheses pool phage therapy outcomes in resistant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* into overall success proportions without examining their aggregability.

**Objective.** To delimit the available evidence and determine whether its structure and reporting admit a quantitative synthesis of efficacy.

**Methodology.** Systematic review following PRISMA 2020, with separate streams for bibliographic databases and trial registries. Eight sources were queried with a 2016-2026 window where the interface allows it. The unit of inclusion was the study, with its full article (a criterion added after extraction). Extraction was in duplicate and independent, with 539 of 541 disagreements adjudicated. Risk of bias was assessed by consensus, by domain, in the 9 comparative studies (RoB 2, ROBINS-I); GRADE was not applied.

**Results.** From 23 057 records, 17 129 unique reports remained; 233 reports formed 183 studies assessed for eligibility, of which 118 were excluded, 63 for lacking the full article. 65 studies remain, all read in full text; requiring it left out 7 of 9 comparative designs. Of these, 52.3 % are single case reports and 3.1 % have a comparative design by abstract; resistance category cannot be assigned from the abstract in 70.8 %. In 43 of the 73 extracted arms (58.9 %) no operational definition of clinical success is on record. No arm meets all seven requirements of a pooled proportion; of the 19 meeting the other six, 14 have a single-patient denominator.

**Conclusions.** The evidence base is broad but unsuited to a quantitative synthesis of efficacy: the published proportions rest on assumptions that these data do not verify.

**Keywords:** bacteriophages; phage therapy; multiple bacterial drug resistance; *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*; systematic review.
